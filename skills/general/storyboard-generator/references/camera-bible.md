# Camera Bible — Storyboard Edition

Complete reference for selecting the right shot type, angle, lens, and lighting
for each storyboard panel based on the emotional intent of the moment.

---

## Shot Types by Distance

| Shot | Abbreviation | Frame Coverage | Use For |
|---|---|---|---|
| Extreme Wide | EWS | Entire landscape, character tiny | Scale, isolation, world-building |
| Wide | WS | Full body + generous environment | Establishing, action choreography |
| Medium Wide | MWS | Knees up | Walking, group dynamics |
| Medium | MS | Waist up | Dialogue, standard coverage |
| Medium Close-Up | MCU | Chest up | Emotion + environment context |
| Close-Up | CU | Face fills frame | Emotion, reaction, intimacy |
| Extreme Close-Up | ECU | Single feature (eye, hand, object) | Tension, detail, significance |

---

## Camera Angles and Power

| Angle | Effect | Use For |
|---|---|---|
| Eye Level | Neutral, relatable | Default — no power statement |
| Low Angle (looking up) | Subject gains power, authority, threat | Heroes, villains, dominance |
| High Angle (looking down) | Subject diminished, vulnerable, trapped | Victims, defeat, overview |
| Dutch / Canted (tilted) | Instability, unease, energy | Chaos, madness, speed |
| Bird's Eye (directly above) | God-view, pattern, detachment | Maps, aftermath, symmetry |
| Worm's Eye (from ground) | Extreme power, towering, otherworldly | Giants, buildings, intimidation |
| Over-the-Shoulder | Subjective viewpoint, confrontation | Dialogue, reveals, stalking |

## Lens Focal Length and Emotional Impact

| Focal Length | Look | Emotional Effect |
|---|---|---|
| 14-18mm (Ultra Wide) | Extreme distortion, vast space | Disorientation, epic scale, surreal |
| 24mm (Wide) | Slight distortion, environmental | Action, establishing, immersion |
| 35mm (Standard Wide) | Natural wide, mild perspective | Documentary feel, honest, grounded |
| 50mm (Normal) | Closest to human eye | Neutral, natural, intimate |
| 85mm (Short Tele) | Slight compression, shallow DoF | Portrait, beauty, isolation of subject |
| 135mm (Medium Tele) | Background compression, soft bokeh | Glamour, voyeurism, distance |
| 200mm+ (Long Tele) | Flat compression, stacked layers | Surveillance, claustrophobia, heat shimmer |

## Lighting Setups for Storyboard Panels

| Setup | Description | Mood |
|---|---|---|
| High-key | Bright, even, minimal shadows | Optimism, safety, commercial |
| Low-key | Deep shadows, selective highlights | Danger, mystery, noir |
| Rembrandt | Triangle of light on shadow-side cheek | Classic portrait, dignity |
| Split | Half face lit, half dark | Duality, moral conflict |
| Backlit / Silhouette | Light behind subject | Mystery, anonymity, grandeur |
| Practical | In-scene light sources (lamps, screens) | Realism, atmosphere, night |
| Golden Hour | Warm, low-angle natural light | Romance, nostalgia, beauty |
| Overcast / Diffused | Soft, even, no hard shadows | Melancholy, thought, neutrality |

## Camera Movement Motivations

| Movement | Emotional Meaning | Use When |
|---|---|---|
| Static / Locked | Authority, observation, indifference | Power statement, composed moments |
| Dolly In | Tension building, approaching truth | Character realization, threat reveal |
| Dolly Out | Isolation, abandonment, ending | Character left alone, consequences |
| Pan | Following action, connecting spaces | Transitions, reveals, tracking subject |
| Tilt Up | Aspiration, power, awe | Looking at something towering |
| Tilt Down | Defeat, discovery, gravity | Finding something on ground |
| Tracking | Energy, momentum, journey | Chase, walk-and-talk, pursuit |
| Crane Up | Transcendence, farewell, rising above | Endings, moments of clarity |
| Crane Down | Arrival, descent, god-to-mortal | Introductions, entering a space |
| Orbit | Wonder, examination, revelation | Product reveal, character study |
| Handheld | Documentary truth, chaos, urgency | Combat, panic, intimate realism |
| Whip Pan | Time cut, energy burst, surprise | Scene transitions, shock |

## Composition Rules for Multi-Panel Storyboards

1. **Rule of Thirds** — Place key subjects at intersection points
2. **Leading Lines** — Architecture/environment guides eye to subject
3. **Depth Layers** — Always have foreground + subject + background separation
4. **Eye-line Match** — If character looks right in panel N, what they see goes in panel N+1
5. **180° Rule** — Keep characters on the same side across panels unless motivated to cross
6. **Screen Direction** — Movement left-to-right = progress; right-to-left = return/retreat
7. **Headroom** — More headroom = vulnerability; less = pressure
8. **Nose Room** — Characters facing into empty space = forward momentum; facing frame edge = trapped

## Motion Categories → Camera Selection (Auto-Select Logic)

Use this to automatically pick the right camera setup based on what's happening:

| Motion Category | Default Shot | Default Angle | Default Movement |
|---|---|---|---|
| Dialogue / Confrontation | MCU to CU | Eye level, slight low on dominant | Static or slow dolly in |
| Chase / Pursuit | Wide to Medium | Side profile, low tracking | Fast tracking |
| Fight / Combat | Medium | Low angle, Dutch for chaos | Handheld + whip pans |
| Reveal / Discovery | OTS → Wide | Behind character → eye level | Dolly out or pan |
| Emotional Moment | CU to ECU | Eye level | Static or very slow dolly in |
| Power Display | Low-angle Medium | Looking up at subject | Slow tilt up or static |
| Establishing / World | EWS to WS | Eye level or bird's eye | Static or slow pan |
| Product / Object | MCU to CU | Slight overhead or eye level | Slow orbit |
| Transition / Time-skip | Whip pan or ECU detail | Any | Whip pan |
